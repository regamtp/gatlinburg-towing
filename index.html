Roads That Change the Tow

A tow in Gatlinburg is decided by the road. The Parkway, the Spur, the Bypass, and Newfound Gap Road are all part of the US-441 corridor, and they do not follow the same rules for a wrecker.

The Parkway through town

Through Gatlinburg the Parkway is a four-lane undivided street. The West Prong of the Little Pigeon River holds one side and the storefronts hold the other, so a stopped car is usually sitting in a travel lane. The city numbers the signals. Traffic Light No. 3 is East Parkway, where US-321 meets the Parkway. Traffic Light No. 10 is Ski Mountain Road. A light number, or a business you can read from the driver's seat, puts a truck on the right block. A pin that hops between the river and the sidewalk does not. Our Parkway page is the longer version of that through-road.

The Spur

North of downtown, US-441 and US-321 leave the street grid and become the Spur, a little over four miles of divided highway between the north end of Gatlinburg and Pigeon Forge. It runs on a narrow strip of National Park Service land along the river. The Park Service prohibits commercial vehicles on park roads, and the Spur is the exception. That is the truck route. A car stopped in a through lane here backs up the only practical link a wrecker can use. Say whether you can see the Gatlinburg Welcome Center or which town you are closer to. Details are on the Spur page.

The Bypass is not a shortcut

The Gatlinburg Bypass is a separate 3.6-mile, two-lane road, also kept by the Park Service. Passenger cars use it to skip downtown, from the Spur to Newfound Gap Road near Sugarlands. Trucks cannot. It passes above Ski Mountain Road without a turn onto that climb, and Campbell Lead crosses it without merging. If you are broken down on the Bypass, say which end you are nearer, or that you are in an overlook. A recovery there still has to be cleared with the Park Service. We do not treat the Bypass as a way around the strip.

Newfound Gap Road

South of town, US-441 enters the national park at Sugarlands and becomes Newfound Gap Road. The Park Service describes it as two lanes with continuous grades, tight curves, no truck lanes, and no runaway truck ramps. Commercial vehicles are prohibited, and the Park Service warns that violators can be cited and fined up to $5,000. There is no gas station, no charging station, and no restaurant on the climb. Newfound Gap is the low point of that ridge and still sits at 5,046 feet on the Tennessee–North Carolina line. Name the pull-off if you can: Carlos Campbell Overlook, the Chimneys, Morton Overlook, the Alum Cave trailhead, or the gap parking area where the Appalachian Trail crosses. Cell service on that road is unreliable. The road to Kuwohi, which many GPS units still call Clingmans Dome, leaves near the gap and closes for the winter. If you are on that spur, say so. It is not the main road.

Cabin Roads Above Town

Above the Parkway, the limiting factor is the grade and whether a truck can turn around. Gatlinburg Towing treats those calls as mountain towing, and the truck is chosen before it leaves 715 East Parkway.

Ski Mountain Road

Ski Mountain Road leaves the Parkway at Light 10 and climbs about three miles to the Ober Mountain base, near 2,682 feet. The ski area is up there, and the aerial tram from downtown lands at the same base, so the upper road carries buses in summer and ski traffic in season. Chalet Village and the Baskins Creek streets turn off the climb and narrow right away. A highway flatbed that cannot turn around at the cabin is the wrong truck. A wheel-lift from a pull-off, and a winch from a drive that will not hold the truck, are different jobs. The winch job is quoted before a cable comes out.

Wiley Oakley Drive

Wiley Oakley Drive is the other climb guests learn by name. It leaves the Spur near the Gatlinburg Welcome Center and winds up toward Ski Mountain Road through cabin streets above town, including the Chalet Village side. The public road is paved, steep, and curvy, and some of the bends are hard to see around. The private drives past the mailboxes are steeper, and some end at a pad sized for two cars. Say whether the vehicle is on Wiley Oakley itself or on gravel past the last house, and whether it will roll. Dragging a car that is already pointed downhill is how a recovery turns into body damage.

Glades Road and the Arts and Crafts loop

East of downtown, the Great Smoky Arts and Crafts Community is an eight-mile loop. From the Parkway, East Parkway (US-321) runs about three miles to Glades Road. The loop follows Glades Road to Buckhorn Road and returns to East Parkway. The city's Yellow Route trolley serves that Glades and Buckhorn stretch. Grades are real, the road is narrow, and in summer people park tight against the studios. If you cannot tell the roads apart, read the nearest studio sign. A car that overheated on the uphill side of Glades needs a truck that can hold it, not a push from the car behind. The Arts and Crafts loop page covers the same roads.

Ice, Snow, and the Climb into the Park

Elevation is the winter problem, not the date on a calendar. The Park Service does not salt roads inside the park. Crews plow and spread sand and gravel. Rain in Gatlinburg can be snow at Newfound Gap, and higher secondary roads close for the season even when the Parkway is only wet pavement.

A car that climbed Ski Mountain Road in the afternoon can be stopped on ice after dark. Shaded drives off Wiley Oakley and Glades Road sit above the strip, so they can still be icy after the Parkway has thawed. If the car is off the pavement and will not roll, say that first. That is a recovery. If it only sat overnight and will not crank, a jump start is often the whole call. If the tank went empty because the last pump was in town, gas delivery is enough fuel to reach a station, and only where a truck is allowed to go. Inside the park, that permission belongs to the Park Service.

An RV that quits on the Spur is a different truck from a passenger car. Tell us the length before anyone is dispatched. Camper and RV towing is a regular call on that highway in season, and a rig that will not fit a campground loop needs the same description. If another vehicle is involved, leave both where they are until the people on scene say they can move, and call 911 if anyone is hurt. Accident towing is the call for a car that cannot leave on its own.

What to Have Ready

Call (865) 277-9413 when the car is in a lane or on a grade. The form on this page is for a quote or a follow-up, not for a vehicle blocking traffic. These four details make the first call useful.

  1. 1

    The place

    Road name, numbered traffic light, business, studio, overlook, or campground loop. Add which way the vehicle is facing, and whether it is in a travel lane, a pull-off, a parking deck, or off the pavement.

  2. 2

    The vehicle

    Car, truck, motorcycle, or RV. If it is a motorhome or a trailer, give the length. A passenger-car flatbed and a truck rated for a camper are not the same dispatch.

  3. 3

    The job

    A tow, a spare, fuel, a jump, a lockout, or a winch because it will not roll. Say which one. A flat on a level pull-off with a real spare is a tire change. A missing spare, or a car on a steep pitch, is a tow.

  4. 4

    Where it should go

    Back to the cabin, a shop in Gatlinburg, or farther on. Long-distance towing is priced for the whole trip before the vehicle is loaded, so the destination is part of the quote.

If someone is hurt, call 911 before you call a tow truck. Gatlinburg has no hospital. Gatlinburg Fire Department headquarters is at 1230 East Parkway. Urgent Care of the Smokies, at 1015 East Parkway, sees problems that are not life-threatening. It is not an emergency room. The 24-hour ER is LeConte Medical Center at 742 Middle Creek Road in Sevierville. We move the vehicle after the scene is safe. We do not transport patients.

Private property towing is a different request. A hotel, a cabin manager, or an attraction lot has to authorize the removal. A neighbor's complaint is not that authorization. Call and we will say what we need before anyone hooks the car.

What to Do While You Wait

Hazards on. If you can leave the travel lane without stepping into traffic, do it. Downtown, the Parkway often has no shoulder between the river and the storefronts. Stay belted in the car until there is a gap, or step to the sidewalk if you can reach it without crossing a lane. Do not stand in the lane to wave drivers around you.

Do not let anyone push a car on Ski Mountain Road, Wiley Oakley Drive, or Glades Road. A vehicle that rolls backward on those grades is a second crash. On Newfound Gap Road, stay with the car and out of the lane. If you have no signal and a ranger or another driver does, ask them to pass the overlook name. On the Spur, move as far right as the shoulder allows.

In a downtown parking deck, stop cranking a dead battery. Note the level and the clearance bar if you can see one. A flatbed that will not fit under the beam is the wrong truck, and a wheel-lift is what we send for a lockout or a dead battery in a low deck. For a straightforward emergency tow in a travel lane, the same rule applies as on the cabin roads: tell us where the car is sitting so the first truck is the right one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast can you get here?

Response time depends on where you are and what's happening on the roads, but because we're local to Gatlinburg, we're usually close by. Call and we'll give you a realistic time the moment we know your location.

Do you tow at night and on weekends?

Yes. We run 24 hours a day, seven days a week, 365 days a year. Breakdowns don't keep business hours, and neither do we.

What kinds of vehicles can you tow?

Cars, trucks, SUVs, motorcycles, RVs, and larger equipment. If you're not sure we can handle your vehicle, call and describe it — we'll tell you straight.

Will you tell me the price before you start?

Yes. We give you an upfront price based on your location, your vehicle, and where it's going. No surprise charges once the job is done.

Can a wrecker take the Bypass around Parkway traffic?

No. The Gatlinburg Bypass is a 3.6-mile National Park Service road, and trucks and other commercial vehicles are prohibited on it. We come by the Parkway or the Spur.

I am past Sugarlands on Newfound Gap Road. Can you drive straight up?

Commercial vehicles are prohibited on Newfound Gap Road. There is no fuel on the mountain, and there are no truck lanes. A breakdown past the entrance has to be coordinated with the Park Service, which changes the timing. Give us the last overlook or trailhead if the call connects.

The driveway off Wiley Oakley or Ski Mountain is too steep. What do you need to know?

The public road, then whether you are still on pavement or on a gravel drive, and whether the car will roll. Many cabin pads have no room to turn a flatbed around. Mountain towing is the winch job, and we quote it before we start.

Do you come out for gas, a jump, or a flat, or only for a tow?

All of those are separate calls: gas delivery, a jump start, and a flat tire change. Say which one you need. There is no fuel sold inside the national park. A fuel stop on Newfound Gap Road happens only if the Park Service lets the truck in and the car is otherwise able to drive.

What if the car is in a lot and I do not own the property?

That is private property towing, and the property has to authorize it. Call and we will tell you what we need before anyone hooks the car.

Who handles injuries?

Call 911. We tow the vehicle. We do not carry patients. Gatlinburg has no hospital. The 24-hour ER is LeConte Medical Center at 742 Middle Creek Road in Sevierville.
